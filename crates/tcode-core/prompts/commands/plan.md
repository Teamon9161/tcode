Inspect whatever I named before you plan, not as a phase inside it: a plan that schedules its own research is not yet a plan. What I want is a concrete, executable plan — one phase per real unit of work.

Write the whole plan into `progress`, not just its checklist. A plan is a document: most of what you learned is not about any one phase. `description` is one line saying what this plan is for. `background` is the plan's prose, in whatever `##` sections this task actually needs — what you decided and why, the alternatives you ruled out and what ruled them out, the facts your investigation established (measurements, versions, what a file actually does today), the shape of the data everything else follows from, the constraints that hold throughout. Do not write a separate markdown file for any of this: a plan split in two is one that stops being current.

Then write every phase's `detail` out in full, and expect that to run long. What you learned while investigating exists nowhere but this conversation, and this conversation is not necessarily what executes the plan — I may hand it to a session that has the file and nothing else. Writing the plan rather than a transcript of your exploration means dropping the narration — what you searched, what you opened — never the facts it turned up; a fact you leave out is one the executor pays to find again.
